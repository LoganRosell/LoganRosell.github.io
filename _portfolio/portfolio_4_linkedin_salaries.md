## Executive Summary & Outcomes

Salary transparency across online job platforms is notoriously inconsistent, with roughly 71% of postings in the LinkedIn dataset omitting normalized salary data entirely. This project developed an end-to-end classification pipeline to predict a job's macroeconomic compensation tier from unstructured text (descriptions, job titles, company names), categorical metadata, and posting engagement.

After evaluating four architectures, the **Random Forest Classifier** emerged as the superior model, achieving a holdout **Cohen's Kappa of 0.624**. The model demonstrated that tree-based ensembles are far better suited than linear, probabilistic, or neural network architectures for handling the extreme sparsity generated by one-hot encoded metadata and TF-IDF text features.

### Key Outcomes:
* **Overcoming Sparsity:** Linear models, Gaussian Naive Bayes, and PyTorch Neural Networks struggled with sparse zero-heavy text features. Random Forest natively handled the high dimensionality, providing the only reliable classification boundaries.
* **Macro Benchmark Binning:** Collapsing continuous compensation into three macroeconomic tiers based on 2024 U.S. median household income resolved neighbor-class confusion from finer-grained splits.
* **Feature Importance Hierarchy:** Extracted PCA components, `pay_period` structures (e.g., yearly vs. hourly), experience level flags, and keyword indicators in job descriptions (`years`, `customer`) served as the strongest drivers of salary tier separation.

## Problem Framing & Target Formulation

* **Initial Goal vs. Final Scope:** Initial attempts to model job posting duration were discarded due to high missingness. The problem was reframed into macro compensation tier classification.
* **Target Metric:** Normalized annual salary partitioned into three tiers using 2024 U.S. median household income benchmarks ($$<\frac{2}{3}\times$$ median for lower; $$>2\times$$ median for upper):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\textbf{Lower Tier:} \quad &\le \$55,819 \\
\textbf{Middle Tier:} \quad &\$55,820 \text{ to } \$167,459 \\
\textbf{Upper Tier:} \quad &\ge \$167,460
\end{aligned}
$$

## Data Cleaning & Feature Pipeline

### 1. Cleaning & Outlier Trimming
Out of 123,849 raw postings, only entries with reported salary data were retained. We trimmed observations below the 3rd percentile and above the 99th percentile to remove reporting anomalies.

  <img src="/images/linkedin_salaries/normalized_salary_distribution_after_trimming.png" alt="Normalized Salary Distribution After Trimming" width="700" />
</p>

### 2. Feature Engineering & High-Dimensional Representations
* **Text (NLP):** Extracted TF-IDF unigrams and bigrams from `company_name`, `title`, and `description` (filtering out stop words and keeping the top 100 features).
* **Categorical Encoding:** One-hot encoded `pay_period`, `formatted_work_type`, `application_type`, `formatted_experience_level`, and two-letter state codes extracted from `location`.
* **Numeric Imputation:** Median-imputed `views` to prevent right-tail skew from corrupting mean estimates.
* **Dimensionality Reduction & Screening:** 
  * Extracted 5 Principal Components (PCA) from the combined feature matrix.
  * Screened variables using continuous OLS linear regression ($$p < 0.05$$) to retain statistically significant predictors prior to classification.

## Modeling & Benchmark Results

Models were trained on 75% of the data and evaluated on a 25% stratified holdout test set using **Cohen's Kappa** to account for class distribution.

### 1. Model Comparisons

| Model | Architecture | Holdout Kappa | Verdict & Trade-offs |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Random Forest** | Tree Ensemble | **0.624** | **Top Performer.** Natively modeled non-linear interactions across sparse text and metadata. |
| **PyTorch Neural Net** | Multi-Layer Perceptron | Moderate | Used class-weighted Cross-Entropy loss, but struggled with sparse feature representations. |
| **Logistic Regression** | Linear (Newton-CG) | Low / Moderate | Failed to capture non-linear boundary splits across text n-grams. |
| **Gaussian Naive Bayes** | Probabilistic | Lowest | Violated independence assumptions due to dense correlations among PCA and text features. |

## Discussion & Key Takeaways

* **Tree Ensembles vs. Deep Learning on Tabular-Text Hybrids:** For tabular datasets augmented with sparse bag-of-words or TF-IDF representations, tree-based models significantly outperform small neural networks and linear baselines without requiring complex embeddings.
* **Impact of Missingness:** High missingness (71% in salary, extensive missingness in skill descriptions) severely restricts usable training volume, demonstrating that platform-wide salary transparency requires structural compensation data rather than voluntary reporting.
